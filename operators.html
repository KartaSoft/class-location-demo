Running this on your line

The map shows the method on public data. On your system it runs on your data, on a schedule, and reports what changed.

What we need from you

  • Centerline. Your GIS centerline with stationing or mileposts, in any GIS format, and the segmentation you report class locations on.
  • Structures. What you already hold: class location study records, structure survey points with occupancy type and dwelling units, aerial patrol notes. Where you hold nothing, we start from public footprints and appraisal records, as on this map.
  • Pipe attributes per segment. MAOP, diameter, wall, grade, seam type, installation year, test records with pressure and duration, in-line inspection reach, leak and rupture history. These turn each class change into its 192.609 study and 192.611 options. On this map they are synthetic.
  • Counties. The counties your line crosses. We collect parcels, plats and permits from the appraisal districts and cities and keep collecting on a schedule.
  • Your current class designations, to reconcile against on the first run.
  • A cadence. Monthly or quarterly, or annual where 192.611(a)(4) applies.

What you get

  • The class location of every segment, with a record of how it was determined (192.5(d)).
  • After each run, the list of changes: new class, study year, confirmation deadline, and the options under 192.611 with their numbers.
  • A watch list of stretches close to the next class, with the probability of a change within one, two and five years.
  • The layers in your GIS format.

Where the data live

In your environment or in an account set up for you. Nothing is published.
